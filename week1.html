Week 1 Homework: What is Creative Coding?

Project chosen: SEEING BIRDSONG by Lucio Arese


Part 1: Project Description

SEEING BIRDSONG is a project designed and developed by Lucio Arese to preserve the complex acoustic of bird vocalization through visualization. He turned self-recorded audio sample into higher dimensional vectors that describes the spectral content of the audio, which is then collapsed and expressed through 2D and 3D graphs.

As a result, different types of bird vocalization traces a special structure of sound on the visual map due to their distinct tonal quality and frequency combinations.

Part 2: Why This Project Is Interesting

To put it most straightforwardly, this project is a creative approach to express one human sensory through another. It essentially turns something we can only hear, the bird vocalizations, into dots, lines and thus trends that we can see on a screen. Not only the transposition itself is meaningful, the immense amount of information carried by the visual is too. This project diversed our analytical lens when it comes to acoustic data processing by simultaneously applying and visually present multiple types of spectral analysis in real time.

Personally speaking, this projects presented me a brand new scope of where my area of study can land - in a way that's both information rich and artistically expressive; something that has intrinsic meaning in itself but can also be used for scientific research purpose.

More technically speaking, this project is a new scope of audio analysis. It transformed the convention audio data presentation from 2D spectrogram to 3D manifold specifically programmed to represent audio content. Specifically, Lucio recently posted a simulation that includes four wren samples recorded from different part of the province - different locations, essentially. He compared them under the units of spectral centroid, chroma energy concentration, spectral spread, spectral skewness, spectral flux, tonality, spectral contrast, spectral crest, and spectral entropy. These different scopes essentially includes observations on the frequency content characteristics, energy distribution characteristics, and tonal characteristics of the sample. It's fascinating to know that he was able to visualize the result of all these analysis under their corresponding units, through color mapping. This processing can bring huge potential, as we are then able to further investigate the difference in these parameters amongst same specie of bird from different area.

As I mentioned, this project ignited a new way of both research approach and artistic expression. We are able to see the difference in the shapes of manifolds from different kinds of bird vocalization and compare the pattern to reach interesting observations and conclusions. As Lucio would put it, “It's essentially a new interface for data inspection.” On the other hand, it's just purely aesthetically pleasing to watch the algorithm processes bird audio into logical visuals in real time and observe patterns and repetitions.

Every technical analysis in this project is achieved through coding, the digital signal processing streamline. However, one element that makes it creative, and thus interesting to me, is where the idea is originated and inspired. As Lucio mentioned, this is an experimental project aims to introduce new ways of preserving the complexity of bird vocalizations beyond just an audio waveform of time and amplitude. The intention itself isn't technical, but instead originates from a love towards nature, or even a love towards exploring the unknown patterns and properties of the nature. When a project is so deeply driven by the nature of curiosity and the spirit of exploration, the action would be intertwined with creativity, so to create the results I'm able to see today. And that's what's beautiful and exciting about “creative coding” - it's the implementation of technicality creatively to reach an objective prompted by human nature.
